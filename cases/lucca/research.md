## CONFIRMED FACTS

### Origin
- Female. Usually given as a **German shepherd–Belgian Malinois mix** (Wikipedia; Animals in War & Peace). Her tag number was **K458**.
- Born about early 2004. DVIDS called her "8-year-old" in July 2012 and "12-year-old" in Feb 2016. She died in Jan 2018, a few weeks before turning 14 (Animals in War & Peace).
- She was a **Specialized Search Dog** (SSD), trained to search off-leash at a distance ahead of her handler (Wikipedia; PDSA describes "search and explosives dog").
- Her original handler and trainer was **Gunnery Sgt. Christopher "Chris" Willingham**, USMC, from Tuscaloosa, Alabama (AP; DVIDS: "Lucca's original trainer").

### Service
- **Iraq, with Willingham, two tours** (PDSA press release):
  - 2007: clearing operations along the **Tigris River, south of Baghdad**.
  - 2008: counter-insurgency operations around **Diwaniyah**.
  - Her finds included weapons caches, IEDs and bombs. PDSA says she was "directly responsible for the apprehension of four insurgents."
- **Afghanistan, with Cpl. Juan Rodriguez**:
  - In Nov 2011, Lucca and Rodriguez were selected from 60 military working dog teams to deploy to Afghanistan with the Marine Expeditionary Force (PDSA).
  - They led **75 patrols in Helmand Province** (PDSA), at the front so that the troops behind them were safe.
  - **7 Mar 2012:** she indicated a 30 lb pressure-plate IED directly in the patrol's path. Rodriguez halted the patrol, and the device was cleared (PDSA).
- **Totals:** "**more than 400 missions**" (PDSA; NBC; AP; CNN), also described as "approximately 400 patrols" (USMC/DVIDS 2016). "Six years of active service" (PDSA). DVIDS 2012 credits her with uncovering "more than 40 IEDs"; Animals in War & Peace says "at least 40 confirmed finds."
- **No human casualties on any patrol she led** (PDSA; AP; USMC/DVIDS: "No Marines were injured on any patrol she led, including her final patrol").
- Unit at retirement: 1st Law Enforcement Battalion, I Marine Expeditionary Force, Camp Pendleton (DVIDS 2012 caption).

### The final patrol: 23 March 2012, Helmand Province
(PDSA; CNN; NBC. Keep it non-graphic.)
- She found a weapons cache hidden in a haystack in a poppy field, then a second 30 lb pressure-plate IED, which was cleared.
- As she searched a nearby path, a third device went off beneath her. She lost her **left front leg** and had burns to her chest (PDSA). SAFE on-screen wording: "An explosive device went off beneath her. She survived, but lost her front left leg."
- Rodriguez ran to her, carried her to the cover of a tree line, applied a tourniquet and called for a medevac (PDSA quote below).
- **No one else in the patrol was hurt** (PDSA: "Miraculously, none of the soldiers in the patrol were injured").
- She was evacuated to **Germany**, then to **Camp Pendleton, California**. Rodriguez stayed with her at every stage, sometimes sleeping next to her (PDSA; NBC).
- **She was up and walking within ten days** (PDSA).

### Retirement
- On 2 July 2012 at Camp Pendleton, the Marines photographed her and filmed a video titled "Homeward Bound Lucca Retires" (DVIDS, Cpl. Jennifer Pirante). Rodriguez was to escort her to Willingham. American Airlines and Air Compassion for Veterans flew them and held an honor ceremony during a stop at O'Hare Airport in Chicago (DVIDS caption). The caption says Willingham was then based in "Finland". This comes from a single source, so don't use it on screen.
- She was adopted by Willingham and lived with his family in California (PDSA; AP; CNN).
- Sep 2013: she walked in the Tunnel to Towers run in New York (MTA photo on Commons, CC BY 2.0).

### Recognition
- **5 April 2016: PDSA Dickin Medal**, at **Wellington Barracks, London**. The PDSA calls it "the animal equivalent of the Victoria Cross". It was presented by PDSA Director General Jan McLoughlin. She was **the first U.S. Marine Corps dog** to receive it (PDSA; AP; NBC; CNN). Willingham was with her in London.
- AP note: the PDSA is a veterinary charity, not part of the British military. The medal is "not officially connected to the Victoria Cross" (Marine Corps Times/AP).
- 14 Nov 2019: received, after her death, one of the first Animals in War & Peace **Medals of Bravery** (No. 6), at a Capitol Hill ceremony (Animals in War & Peace; Washington Post KidsPost, 18 Nov 2019, via Wikipedia).
- Book: *Top Dog: The Story of Marine Hero Lucca* by Maria Goodavage (2014).

### Death and legacy
- **Died 20 January 2018** in California, aged nearly 14. Willingham's family and Rodriguez were with her (Animals in War & Peace). Willingham himself confirms the year: "Lucca died in 2018" (US War Dogs Association interview). SAFE: "She died peacefully in January 2018, surrounded by the people who loved her." The cause was old age (Inside Edition). Some sources say a decision was made so she would not suffer; don't dramatize it.
- **July 2021:** her ashes were interred with full honors at the **Michigan War Dog Memorial** in South Lyon, Michigan. Willingham (by then a retired Master Sergeant) carried her ashes, and Rodriguez attended (Inside Edition; US War Dogs Association).
- A bronze statue of Lucca, titled "Send Me", stands at the National Cowgirl Museum and Hall of Fame sculpture garden in Fort Worth, Texas. A second was dedicated on 13 Mar 2025 at Veterans Military Park in Tuscaloosa, Alabama (Animals in War & Peace only; NOT independently verified, so check before using).
- Willingham went on to become president of the US War Dogs Association (uswardogs.org).

## QUOTES (exact, all from the PDSA release of 5 Apr 2016; repeated by NBC and CNN)
- Rodriguez: "The explosion was huge and I immediately feared the worst for Lucca. I ran to her and saw her struggling to get up. I picked her up and ran to the shelter of a nearby tree line; applied a tourniquet to her injured leg and called the medics to collect us."
- Rodriguez: "She had saved my life on so many occasions – I had to make sure that I was there for her when she needed me."
- Rodriguez: "Her fighting spirit was plain to see and I was so proud of how quickly she recovered."
- Willingham: "Lucca is very intelligent, loyal and had an amazing drive for work as a search dog. She is the only reason I made it home to my family and I am fortunate to have served with her."
- Willingham: "Today, I do my best to keep her spoiled in her well-deserved retirement."
- Willingham (Inside Edition, 2021): "Lucca saved my life on multiple occasions."
- Willingham (US War Dogs Association): "We both shed some tears for her again that day." (said of the 2021 interment with Rodriguez)

## DISPUTED POINTS: safest wording
- **Breed:** DVIDS (2012) says "Belgian Malinois"; PDSA, AP and CNN say "German Shepherd"; Wikipedia and Animals in War & Peace say a mix. SAFE: "a German shepherd–Malinois mix."
- **Which Dickin Medal she was:** the PDSA press release says "66th", but the PDSA award page and Animals in War & Peace say "67th". SAFE: don't give a number; say "one of fewer than 70 animals ever awarded it" or skip.
- **"First US dog"**: CENTCOM said the first US military working dog; the PDSA said the first US **Marine Corps** dog. Use the PDSA wording.
- **Deployments:** PDSA gives two tours in Iraq (with Willingham) and one in Afghanistan (with Rodriguez), matching DVIDS. Animals in War & Peace says "three combat tours" in total. SAFE: "three combat deployments: two to Iraq, one to Afghanistan."
- **400 missions vs 400 patrols:** say "more than 400 missions".
- **Which handler served where:** the PDSA award page wrongly says she served with Willingham in Afghanistan. The detailed PDSA press release and DVIDS say Willingham was her Iraq handler and Rodriguez her Afghanistan handler. Use the latter.
- **Willingham's rank:** Gunnery Sergeant (2012–2016 sources); retired Master Sergeant by 2021 (Inside Edition). Say "Gunnery Sergeant Chris Willingham" for the service years.
- **Death vs burial:** she died in Jan 2018; the Michigan burial was in July 2021. Don't merge them.

## MYTHS AND POPULAR ERRORS to correct on screen
1. **Lucca is not the dog in the viral "Goodbye, my brother" farewell video.** Some outlets conflated her with **Cena**, a Marine dog who died in 2017 (Fox/AP). Her 2021 Michigan burial is also sometimes merged with Cena's story. Never use Cena footage.
2. "Lucca was the first animal / first US animal to get the Dickin Medal." She was the first US **Marine Corps dog**. (The US pigeon G.I. Joe received one in 1946, and the WWII US Army dog Chips received one after his death in 2018.)
3. "She lost her back leg." She lost her **front left** leg (PDSA; DVIDS).
4. "The Dickin Medal is a British military award." It comes from a veterinary charity, the PDSA (AP).
5. "She died in the blast" or "she died in 2016". She survived and lived for nearly six more years.
6. She is sometimes called a "search and rescue dog" (on the PDSA web page). She was an explosives detection and search dog.

## EMOTIONAL PEAK MOMENTS (all true)
1. **Rodriguez never left her side.** He carried her to safety, applied the tourniquet, then stayed with her through Germany and California, sometimes sleeping next to her (PDSA).
2. **Walking in ten days.** On three legs, within ten days of her injury (PDSA).
3. **"She is the only reason I made it home to my family."** Willingham on the dog he trained (PDSA).
4. **London, 2016.** A three-legged Marine dog receives "the animal Victoria Cross" at Wellington Barracks.
5. **The final goodbye.** In 2021 both of her handlers stood together at the Michigan War Dog Memorial as her ashes were laid to rest, and, in Willingham's words, "We both shed some tears for her again that day."
