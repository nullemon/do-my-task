==================== YOUTUBE DESCRIPTION ====================
In September 1918, according to his own account, American airman Lee Duncan found a starving mother dog and her newborn puppies in a shell-wrecked kennel near Flirey, France. He kept two of them and named them Rin Tin Tin and Nanette. Nanette died soon after reaching America, but Rin Tin Tin became one of the biggest movie stars of the 1920s, the hero of more than two dozen films.

This video uses real footage of Rin Tin Tin from his own public-domain silent films. It also sets the record straight: he did not win the first Best Actor Oscar, he did not die in Jean Harlow's arms, and the 1950s TV "Rin Tin Tin" was a different dog.

FACT-CHECK NOTES
• The kennel story comes from Lee Duncan's own telling, and details vary between versions; we say "according to Duncan".
• Birth date: September 1918 (Duncan used Sept 10; some sources give Oct 10).
• Film count: "more than two dozen" (sources give 23 to 27).
• "Saved Warner Bros.": often claimed, no balance sheet proves it; we say he is "often credited with helping keep the studio afloat". Studio insiders called him "the mortgage lifter".
• The first Best Actor Oscar went to Emil Jannings. The Academy's surviving signed ballots do not mention a dog (Bruce Davis, TheWrap).
• He died on 10 August 1932 at Lee Duncan's home in Los Angeles. The Jean Harlow story is a press invention.
• The 1950s TV series used different dogs.
• Film scenes are used to show Rin Tin Tin himself; they are scenes from his movies, not footage of the real-life events described.
