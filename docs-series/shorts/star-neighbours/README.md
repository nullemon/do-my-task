# What If Famous Stars Were Our Nearest Neighbour? (YouTube Short, B7)

The video is 1080×1920 at 30 fps and 38.6 s long, 9:16 only. It uses the batch-2 "swap" format: one locked tripod
shot of the user's `C_city.png` at night with the city lights on, under the real southern sky. The sky is HYG v4.1 at
its true RA/Dec and B−V colours, at latitude 34° S and sidereal time 10.85 h, facing south-east, with the Southern
Cross and the Pointers over the skyline.

Alpha Centauri (4.37 ly) is swapped, one star after another, for famous stars placed at the same distance and the
same point in the sky. Each swap has:
- an implosion of the old star;
- a tinted flash;
- a chromatic shockwave and beams;
- the new star blooming in.

The city is relit by each new star. The ending is a white-out back to Alpha Centauri, so the Short loops. One orange
word is shown at a time, and each star gets a bold readout: NAME / MAGNITUDE · × / comparison. There are no
disclaimer captions.

## Narration (78 words, Kokoro am_michael @ 0.92, real word timings, calm pacing)
There are pauses of ~0.35 s between sentences and ~0.7 s at each beat change.
> What if the brightest stars in our galaxy were our nearest neighbour? Tonight, it's Alpha Centauri. Sirius. As
> bright as Jupiter. Vega. Still just a dot. Betelgeuse. Over a hundred times brighter than Venus. Now the city casts
> shadows. Rigel. Almost a full Moon, from one tiny point. Deneb. As bright as the full Moon. Eta Carinae. In its
> 1843 outburst, over a hundred full Moons. Night turns to twilight. Lucky for us, our neighbours are small.

- **Sources:**
  - HYG v4.1 (Hipparcos) for positions and colours. HYG's own absmag values use Hipparcos distances: Rigel −6.93
    (264 pc) and Deneb −6.93 (433 pc).
  - The M values above are the literature values given in the brief:
    - Rigel −7.84 (Przybilla et al. 2006 and Moravveji et al. 2012).
    - Deneb about −8.4. Schiller & Przybilla 2008 give ~800 pc, so M_V is about −8.3 to −8.4.
  - With the HYG values, Rigel and Deneb would be about −11.3 at 1.34 pc, which is still "almost a full Moon".
  - **Betelgeuse** varies between V ≈ 0.0 and 1.6, so M ranges from about −5.4 to −6. The narration says "over a
    hundred times brighter than Venus", which holds across the whole range.
  - **Eta Carinae:**
    - M_V ≈ −8.6 (2012 value as given in the literature and on Wikipedia).
    - L ≈ 5 × 10⁶ L☉ for the system (the readout says "5 MILLION × THE SUN'S POWER"; Davidson & Humphreys 1997/2012).
    - Distance ~2.3 kpc. It has brightened by ~0.5 mag since 2012, so today it is perhaps −13.5 at 1.34 pc.
    - The 1843 Great Eruption peaked at apparent V ≈ −0.8. With ~2 mag of extinction, that gives M_V ≈ −13.5 to −14.3.
      At 1.34 pc this is m ≈ −17.9 to −18.7, which is 120–250 full Moons. The narration says "over a hundred full
      Moons" and the readout says ~180×.
- **Why Eta Carinae and not R136a1:** R136a1 has L ≈ 4.7–6 × 10⁶ L☉, so M_bol ≈ −11.9 to −12.2. At ~46–50 kK its
  bolometric correction is about −4, so M_V ≈ −8.2 and m ≈ −12.5, which is less than Deneb in visible light; most of
  its output is UV. Eta Carinae's 1843 eruption has the larger, defensible visual figure.
- **"Night turns to twilight":** the full Moon gives ~0.25 lux, so 180× is ~45 lux. That falls within civil twilight
  (about 3–400 lux between the end of civil twilight and sunrise).
- **"Still a point":** every star stays far below the eye's ~1′ resolution, from 8 mas to ~5″ for Betelgeuse. The
  brightness is the spectacle.
  - **Exception:** Eta Carinae's Homunculus nebula. It is ~0.2 pc long (18″ at 2.3 kpc), so from 1.34 pc it would
    span ~8.5°. It is drawn as a faint orange bipolar nebula at that size (~140 px) around the point.
- **Where:** Alpha Centauri (Dec −60.8°) never rises north of ~29° N, so the Short is set in a southern city at 34° S.
  There Alpha Centauri is 45° up in the south-east, with Hadar and the Southern Cross above it.

## Picture (`short.py`)
- **Star:** an HDR core, a halo and 8 chromatic diffraction spikes. The spike length is linear in magnitude, from
  ~130 px at −0.3 to ~1,500 px at −18. The star colour comes from blackbody T. Rainbow dispersion is added along the
  long spikes, and there is a steady beam fan for the brilliant stars. God rays appear above magnitude ~−6.
- **Sky:** a deep-blue city night with an orange light dome, never black, plus a vivid Milky Way and drifting airglow.
  Scattered starlight adds a Rayleigh-blue twilight gradient and a forward-scattering aureole tinted by the star; both
  scale with log(flux / full Moon).
  - The limiting magnitude drops with sky brightness, and there is an extra glare zone around the star, so the faint
    HYG stars wash out. During the eruption the whole field disappears.
- **City:** a light decomposition of the plate (as in `../solar-storm`): the plate with the lights off, window light
  and lamp light. All lights stay on. Window states change a little at every swap, with a staggered delay, and the
  final beat matches the opening.
- **Starlight on the city:**
  - Rim and top light come from marching towards the star in screen space until the sky.
  - The lit surfaces are the left roof, the near flat roof, the street and the brick block's roof line.
  - Crisp cast shadows fall on them: the plant room, the parapet lip, the street trees and two lamp posts. They run
    away from the point on the horizon below the star.
  - From Betelgeuse onwards, the three tall towers cast long, slight shadows onto the lower roofs, in the star's
    direction.
  - Scattered skylight lifts everything at twilight.

## Liberties (deliberately not captioned)
- **Exaggerated light:** the display brightness of the sky and the city is a log mapping, not linear. Under a
  quarter-to-full Moon (Betelgeuse to Eta Carinae) the city would look moonlit to the eye. Here it is lit more
  visibly, and with the star's colour, for the spectacle. The camera exposure is locked, with a gentle compensation
  so that the highlights don't clip.
- **Colours and spikes:**
  - Eta Carinae is drawn blue-white for its hot primary. Today's observed colour is reddened by the Homunculus dust.
  - The colour of the 1843 eruption is simplified to a warm white. Its pseudo-photosphere was F-type.
  - The spikes, beams, shockwaves and implosion/bloom swaps are artistic devices. Diffraction spikes come from the
    camera, not from the sky.
- **Shadows:** the shadows are screen-space approximations on hand-traced surfaces, not a 3-D model of the plate.
  Their direction follows the star's azimuth.
- **Approximate statements:**
  - The plate is a European-looking city standing in for a southern city.
  - "Brightest stars in our galaxy" is rhetorical. Sirius and Vega are famous rather than extreme. Rigel, Deneb,
    Betelgeuse and Eta Carinae are among the most luminous stars known.
  - "Sirius as bright as Jupiter" refers to Jupiter at its best (−2.9).

## Files
- `audio/narration.py`, `audio/gen_voice.py` and `audio/mix.py`:
  - The mix has a chord pad per star that gets brighter and wider with each star.
  - Each swap has a whoosh, an implosion sweep, a sub thump and a bloom shimmer.
  - The eruption has a riser, a blast and a rumble, followed by a twilight shimmer.
  - The ending has a white-out riser and a hit.
  - The mix is at −14 LUFS / −1 dBTP and also writes `out/star-neighbours.srt`.

- Shared code: `../lib/` (unchanged). Scratch: `.../scratchpad/series/B2-star-neighbours/` (HYG csv in `dl/hyg/`).
- Outputs in `out/` (git-ignored): `star-neighbours-short.mp4`, `star-neighbours.srt`, `thumb-vertical.jpg` and
