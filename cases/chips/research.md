## Confirmed facts
- DOG: a mixed breed. German Shepherd–Husky (PDSA, VOA quoting John Wren); German Shepherd–Collie–Husky or Malamute (Army.mil, DMN, NCC). Safest: "a German Shepherd mix". Born about 1940 (Wikipedia; exact date unknown).
- FAMILY: Edward J. Wren and family, Pleasantville, New York (PDSA, Army.mil, DMN, NCC). A neighbouring family gave him to the Wrens as a puppy (VOA). John Wren, the son, recalled: "The runt of the litter was this German Shepherd-husky mix thing." (VOA, exact quote)
- DONATED in 1942 through Dogs for Defense, the civilian programme that recruited family pets for the Army (PDSA, Army.mil, DMN; DMN: enlisted August 1942). NCC (from an obituary source): the family sent him after he bit a garbage collector. Single source, so present it as "family lore" or omit.
- TRAINED at the War Dog Training Center, Front Royal, Virginia (DMN, Military.com, Wikipedia). Trained as a sentry dog.
- HANDLER: Pvt. John Rowell. The middle initial varies (Army.mil, DMN and Military.com have "John R. Rowell"; Wikipedia has "John P."). Say "Private John Rowell". DMN says he was from Arkansas (single source).
- UNIT: 3rd Infantry Division. DMN: 3rd Military Police Platoon, 30th Infantry Regiment. PDSA and VOA say "Third Infantry Regiment of the Seventh Army", which is probably a conflation. Say "the U.S. 3rd Infantry Division".
- NORTH AFRICA: Operation Torch (Nov 1942). Jan 1943, sentry at the Casablanca Conference of Roosevelt and Churchill (PDSA date link, VOA, Army.mil, DMN, NCC).
- SICILY, 10 July 1943 (D-Day of Operation Husky; PDSA, Wikipedia, DMN): the 3rd Division landed near Licata (DMN, Military.com). Pinned down by a machine gun firing from a hut or pillbox, Chips broke from Rowell and charged in. The firing stopped, and a soldier came out with Chips at his throat; three more followed with their hands up (PDSA, DMN, Wikipedia, Army.mil "four enemy soldiers"). Military.com says 9 July, but the landing was 10 July, so use 10 July.
  - Rowell, as quoted in 1943 press (Wikipedia/PDSA): "I called him off before he could kill the man." Use only this short part; it is in PDSA and Wikipedia.
  - Chips had a scalp wound and powder burns from a pistol fired at close range. Medics treated him and he was back the same day (PDSA, DMN, Army.mil).
- THAT NIGHT on guard duty he alerted Rowell to ten Italian soldiers approaching, and all ten were captured (DMN, Military.com, Army.mil "attributed to unnamed sources", NCC). Say "credited with".
- The enemy soldiers were ITALIAN (DMN, Wikipedia, Military.com). VOA's "German" is wrong.
- AWARDS: recommended for the Distinguished Service Cross by Capt. Edward G. Parr (DMN, Military.com). Silver Star and Purple Heart (PDSA, DMN). Maj. Gen. Lucian K. Truscott Jr. presented the award(s) in Italy on 19 Nov 1943 (Army.mil citing a 1944 NYT article; NCC citing AP; PDSA gives 19 Nov 1943 for the Silver Star). DSC citation text (Army.mil): "courageous action in single-handedly eliminating a dangerous machine-gun nest and causing surrender of its crew."
- BACKLASH: William Thomas, national commander of the Military Order of the Purple Heart, protested to President Roosevelt, the Secretary of War and the Adjutant General (DMN, Military.com, NCC). The War Department ruled that no more decorations would go to animals; Adjutant General Maj. Gen. James A. Ulio (Time, Feb 1944, per Wikipedia and NCC).
- His men unofficially gave him a theater ribbon with an arrowhead and battle stars (eight per DMN, Military.com, Wikipedia).
- EISENHOWER: Chips nipped General Eisenhower's hand when Ike reached to pet him (DMN, NCC; Army.mil says "rumored"). Present as "the story goes".
- CAMPAIGNS: North Africa, Sicily, Italy, France, Germany (Army.mil lists Algerian-Moroccan, Tunisian, Sicilian, Rhineland, Central Europe). Served until the end of the war.
- HOME: discharged and returned to the Wrens in Pleasantville in December 1945 (Army.mil, DMN, Military.com). John Wren was about 4 years old (VOA).
- DEATH: 1946, a few months after coming home. NCC: about seven months after discharge. EBSCO: 12 Apr 1946. Army.mil: about a year later, "from war injuries". CBC and others: kidney failure. Safest: "Chips died in 1946, only months after coming home." Do not state the cause, or say "possibly linked to his wartime injuries".
- DICKIN MEDAL: PDSA Dickin Medal (Britain; called "the animals' Victoria Cross"), presented on 15 Jan 2018 at the Churchill War Rooms, London, 75 years after the Casablanca Conference. Chips was the 70th recipient. Accepted by Lt. Col. Alan Throop and U.S. military working dog Ayron. John Wren attended (Army.mil) (PDSA, VOA, Army.mil). John Wren: "It's just a nice way of honoring a war hero." (VOA, exact quote)
- 2019: Animals in War & Peace Medal of Bravery (Wikipedia citing Washington Post 2019; Army.mil).
- The Dickin Medal was moved to the 3rd Infantry Division Museum, Fort Stewart, Georgia, on 13 Apr 2022 (Army.mil, single source). Before that it was at the National WWII Museum, New Orleans.
- FILM: "Chips, the War Dog", a Disney Channel TV movie (premiered 24 Mar 1990). It is fictionalized; do not use its clips.

## Disputed points: safest wording
- WERE THE MEDALS TAKEN AWAY? Army.mil, PDSA, VOA and Military.com say they were revoked or withdrawn. Time (Feb 1944) and a wire report of 16 Feb 1944 (per NCC and Wikipedia) say Ulio let Chips keep his medals but banned future awards to dogs. ON SCREEN: "Then came the backlash. The Army ruled that medals were for people, not dogs, and Chips's honors were officially stripped away, or at least never recognized again." Shorter: "The Army ruled medals were for humans only, and Chips's awards were officially revoked." Add an on-screen note that some 1944 reports say he was allowed to keep them.
- WHICH MEDALS: the Silver Star and Purple Heart are the strongest; the DSC was recommended, and accounts differ on whether it was presented. Say "recommended for the Distinguished Service Cross and awarded the Silver Star and Purple Heart".
- "Most decorated dog of WWII": a popular superlative. Say "often called the most decorated dog of the war".
- Handler's middle initial: R or P. Say "Private John Rowell".
- Pillbox or hut: say "a machine-gun nest in a small hut".
- Prisoner count: 4 from the nest plus 10 that night, credited.

## Myths and popular errors
1. "The first dog to get a Purple Heart was Stubby." Stubby could not have; the modern medal dates from 1932. Chips really was awarded one in 1943, before the ban.
2. "He fought Germans in Sicily." Those were Italian soldiers at the nest.
3. "Chips was a purebred German Shepherd" (as in the Disney film). He was a mix.
4. "He received the Medal of Honor." False.
5. The Dickin Medal is British (PDSA), not a US military award.
6. "He died in combat." No; he came home in Dec 1945 and died in 1946.

## Emotional peaks (true)
1. The family pet from Pleasantville whose family lent him to the Army, and a 4-year-old boy waiting at home.
2. 10 July 1943: under machine-gun fire on a Sicilian beach, he breaks free and charges the gun alone, then comes back wounded and is on guard again that same night.
3. Decorated, then told medals are "for humans", but his own soldiers pin a homemade ribbon with battle stars on him.
4. He comes home in December 1945 to the same family and dies just months later.
5. 2018: 75 years on, the little boy, now John Wren in his late 70s, is in London as Chips finally receives the Dickin Medal.

## Hook ideas (true)
- "In 1943 a family dog from New York charged an enemy machine gun alone and got medals for it. Then the Army took them back, because he was a dog."
- "He guarded Roosevelt and Churchill, captured enemy soldiers, and bit General Eisenhower. Meet Chips."
