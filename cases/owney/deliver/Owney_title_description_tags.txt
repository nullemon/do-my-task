==================== YOUTUBE DESCRIPTION ====================
In 1888 a scruffy terrier mix turned up at the Albany, New York post office. He fell in love with the mailbags, followed them onto the mail trains, and became the unofficial mascot of the Railway Mail Service, riding more than 140,000 miles. Clerks across America added tags to his collar until the Postmaster General gave him a harness to carry them. In 1895 he sailed around the world on a mail steamer from Tacoma.

When he died in 1897, the mail clerks paid to preserve him. Today he is at the Smithsonian's National Postal Museum, and in 2011 he got his own stamp. The film in this video is real 1897–1903 footage of American mail trains and post offices from the Library of Congress.

FACT-CHECK NOTES
• Smithsonian research suggests he probably belonged to an Albany postal clerk who moved away; the "stray on a cold night" version is a later legend.
• Tags: about a thousand reported; 372 survive. He never wore them all at once.
• Miles: "more than 140,000" (sources give 140,000–143,000).
• World trip: "about four months" (sources give 113–132 days). The Japanese passport is reported only by period newspapers.
• He was put down in Toledo in June 1897 after biting a clerk; we keep this non-graphic.
• Film scenes show mail trains and post offices of the period, not Owney himself; his real photos are labelled.
