## Confirmed facts (2+ sources each)
- **Born:** February 1936, at a kennel in Shanghai, China. A liver-and-white English Pointer, first named "Shudi". [TNA, W, IWM grave inscription "Born Shanghai February 1936"]
- **HMS Gnat:** In late 1936 she was bought as mascot for the Royal Navy river gunboat HMS Gnat on the Yangtze. [TNA, W, GOV]. Her gundog training failed, and she often fell overboard. On board she was valued for warning of river pirates and approaching aircraft. [GOV, W]
- **HMS Grasshopper:** In mid-1939 she moved with crew to HMS Grasshopper and was aboard when war with Japan began in Dec 1941. [TNA, W]
- **The sinking:** On 13 Feb 1942 the ships left Singapore just before it fell. On 14 Feb 1942 Japanese aircraft bombed Grasshopper and Dragonfly, and Grasshopper was abandoned. Judy was trapped below decks and rescued by a petty officer (George White, per W). [TNA, W, PDSA]. The grave inscription reads "Wounded 14th February 1942". [IWM]
- **The water:** The survivors were stranded on a small island with no fresh water. Judy dug and found a freshwater spring. [TNA, PDSA, W, GOV]
- **Trek:** By boat via Singkep to Sumatra, then a long overland trek (about 200 miles per W) toward Padang. On the way a crocodile/alligator wounded her (TNA: a 2-inch cut over her left eye; W: her shoulder). They missed the last evacuation ships. [TNA, W, GOV]
- **Captured:** 18 March 1942. [TNA, W]
- **Medan / Gloegoer camp:** Hidden at first. **Frank Williams**, a Leading Aircraftman in the RAF, began sharing his rice ration with her in about Aug 1942, and they became inseparable. [TNA, PDSA, W, GOV]. Frank later recalled: "I realised that even though she was thin, she was a survivor." [TNA]
- **POW registration:** Registered as an official prisoner of war, number **"81A Gloegoer Medan"**. She is the only dog known to have been officially registered as a POW in WWII. [TNA, W, PDSA (number "POW 81A")]. Registration gave her a ration. [TNA, GOV]
- **The hell ship:** In June 1944 the prisoners were shipped out of Belawan (Medan). Dogs were not allowed, so Judy was smuggled aboard in a rice sack and stayed silent. The ship was the **Harugiku Maru (also spelled Harukiku), formerly the Dutch steamer Van Waerwijck** (PDSA/GOV spell it "Van Warwyck"). On **26 June 1944** it was torpedoed in the Strait of Malacca by the British submarine HMS Truculent, whose crew did not know prisoners were aboard. [TNA, PDSA, W, WVW, PDR]
- Frank pushed Judy out through a porthole. In the water she helped struggling men by bringing them floating debris. [TNA, PDSA, W, GOV]. Leonard Williams, a Grasshopper survivor no relation to Frank, said: "A lot of people owe their lives to Judy." [GOV]
- **Reunion:** Survivors were taken to Singapore, then sent back to Sumatra. Frank feared she was dead, but they were reunited at the next camp. He said he "couldn't believe [his] eyes". [TNA, W]
- **The railway:** For about a year the prisoners cut a railway line through the Sumatran jungle (the Pekanbaru railway). [TNA, PDR, W]. Food was scarce and disease common. Keep this non-graphic.
- **Liberation:** Japan surrendered in Aug 1945. Judy was smuggled aboard the troopship *Antenor* to Liverpool and spent 6 months in quarantine (Hackbridge kennels). [TNA, W]
- **Dickin Medal:** On 30 Apr 1946 the PDSA announced she would receive it; the medal was presented on 2 May 1946 (PDSA: "May 1946"). She was Dickin Medal no. 37. [TNA, PDSA, W]. Citation: "For magnificent courage and endurance in Japanese prison camps, which helped to maintain morale among her fellow prisoners, and also for saving many lives through her intelligence and watchfulness." (First part confirmed verbatim by TNA, PDSA and GOV. Wording of the second half from PDSA's paraphrase and W; confirm the exact end on the PDSA page before quoting it in full on screen.)
- **Frank's award:** The PDSA's White Cross of St Giles, for his care of Judy. [TNA, W]
- **Later life:** In 1948 Frank took a post on the government groundnut scheme in East Africa (Tanganyika, now Tanzania), and Judy went with him. [TNA, W]
- **Death:** **17 February 1950**, Tanzania (then Tanganyika), aged nearly 14. She was put to sleep after a tumour. [TNA, W, PDSA ("February 1950"), IWM grave inscription "Died February 1950"]. She was buried in an RAF jacket. Frank built a memorial stone telling her story (IWM photo). [TNA, W]
- **Legacy:** In 2006 the Williams family gave her Dickin Medal and collar to the Imperial War Museum. [TNA, W, DNA India/Yorkshire Post 2006]. Frank's wife Doris said Judy "undoubtedly and repeatedly saved my husband's life". [TNA]

## Disputed points, with the SAFEST wording
1. **Who registered her and when.** TNA and Wikipedia: Frank persuaded the Medan camp commandant (books name him as Col. Banno; W says the commandant was drunk), in about 1942. PDSA and GOV: after the 1944 sinking a Japanese tanker picked her up, guards wanted to kill her, and the *former* Medan commandant registered her. SAY: "Through Frank Williams' efforts, the Japanese camp commandant agreed to enter Judy on the register as an official prisoner of war: number 81A, Gloegoer, Medan." Do not name the commandant or give a date for the registration.
2. **Hell-ship death toll.** TNA says "over 500" died and PDSA says about 200 of 700 survived. Naval and POW-research figures in WVW and PDR give about **154-198 prisoner deaths**, with roughly 1,000+ survivors. SAY: "Hundreds of prisoners were packed below decks. Many did not survive." If a number is needed: "nearly 200 men died."
3. **Ship name spelling.** Say "the Harugiku Maru, a captured Dutch ship" (har-oo-GEE-koo MAH-roo).
4. **Island and spring.** The island's name varies in popular accounts, so say "a small island".
5. **Crocodile wound location** (eye vs shoulder): say "she was wounded by a crocodile."
6. **Birth year.** Some sites say 1937. Use Feb 1936 (TNA, IWM grave, W).
7. **Gunshot, alligator and wild-dog injuries** in the camps (PDSA, GOV): give only as "she survived injuries, illness and near-starvation."
8. **Wembley appearance and BBC broadcast in 1946** are single-source (W). Leave out or attribute.

## Myths / errors to correct
- GOV.UK's death year of **1960 is wrong**. It was **1950** (TNA, PDSA, IWM grave inscription).
- "Over 500 died on the ship" is a common figure, but careful research puts it lower. Use the hedged wording above.
- She was not the only animal POW story, but she is the **only dog known to have been officially registered** as a POW in WWII. Keep the word "officially".
- Frank Williams (RAF) and Leonard Williams (Royal Navy) are **not related**. Don't mix up their quotes.
- The Dickin Medal is often called the "animals' Victoria Cross". That is a nickname: the PDSA's own 1946 letter used it (TNA). It is not an official VC.

## Emotional peak moments (true)
1. A starving prisoner shares his tiny rice ration with a starving dog, and from that day they are never apart. [TNA, PDSA]
2. Smuggled in a rice sack, silent for hours. Then the ship is torpedoed, Frank pushes her out of a porthole, and she swims among the survivors bringing them wreckage to hold. [TNA, PDSA, GOV]
3. Each fears the other is dead, until Frank walks into a new camp and "couldn't believe [his] eyes". [TNA]
- Bonus: on a waterless island, the ship's dog digs and finds fresh water [TNA, PDSA]. Ending: Frank builds her memorial stone with his own hands; the IWM photo shows it.

## Hook (true)
"In the Second World War, thousands of men were registered as prisoners of the Japanese. Only one dog ever was. Her number was 81A."

## Visual notes
- Commons has only two photos that actually show Judy: HU 42990 (on deck) and HU 43988 (her legs beside her puppies). IWM holds more (e.g. Judy with Frank Williams), but they are not on Commons and need IWM licensing. Use breed photos labelled "an English Pointer like Judy".
- Avoid photos of emaciated POWs (graphic). The Commons photo "Monument of the Birma railway" shows the Burma (Thai-Burma) railway, NOT Pekanbaru, so do not use it.
