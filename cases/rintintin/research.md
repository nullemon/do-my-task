## Confirmed facts (2+ sources each)
- **Finder:** Cpl. Leland "Lee" Duncan, an armorer in the US Army Air Service, 135th Aero Squadron. [W, DB, NPR]
- **Discovery:** On 15 Sept 1918, Duncan went to the village of Flirey (Lorraine, France, in the St. Mihiel sector) to scout a possible airfield. There he found a bombed German army kennel, with a starving mother dog and a litter of five puppies whose eyes were still closed. [W, DB]. NOTE: the source for this is Duncan's own account (see Disputed).
- **Name:** Duncan kept two puppies, a male and a female. He named them Rin Tin Tin and Nanette after a pair of good-luck dolls, "Rintintin and Nénette", that French children gave to American soldiers. [W, DB]
- **Breed:** German shepherd. [W, BRIT]
- **Birth:** September 1918, days before Duncan found the litter. Duncan used Sept 10 as the birthday, and Warner Bros. marked the "100th birthday" in Sept 2018. Wikipedia gives Oct 10. Say **"born in September 1918"**, or just "a newborn".
- **Home:** In 1919 Duncan got both puppies onto a ship to the US. Nanette caught pneumonia and died in New York state (Hempstead, NY). Duncan took Rin Tin Tin home to Los Angeles. [W citing ORL pp. 43-44; NPR]
- **Duncan's childhood:** His mother gave him up and he spent years in an orphanage before she took him back. [MAC, PM, both summarising ORL]
- **Discovered by film:** At a 1922 Shepherd Dog Club of California show, a cameraman, Charles Jones, filmed Rinty jumping (reported as about 11 to 12 ft, figure not verified). Duncan decided the dog could act. [W citing ORL pp. 48-49]
- **First film:** *The Man from Hell's River* (1922). He replaced a wolf that would not perform and was billed as "Rin Tan". [W citing NYT 2011 and ORL]
- **Breakthrough:** *Where the North Begins* (Warner Bros., 1923), his first starring role, was a big hit. [W, WTNB, DB]
- **Career:** He made about 27 Hollywood films. Say **"more than two dozen films."** [W; other sources give 23 to 27]
- **Studio nickname:** Warner insiders called him "the mortgage lifter". [W citing ORL p. 81, DB]
- **Fame:** He was popular around the world. A 1925 trade paper shows a Paris cinema playing his film as "Chien Loup", and Sydney cinemas showed him in 1927. [Commons media]
- **Sound era:** Warner dropped him after *The Show of Shows* (1929). He then made serials and features for independent producer Nat Levine (Mascot), including *The Lightning Warrior* (1931). From 1930 he starred in a radio show, *The Wonder Dog*. [W]
- **Death:** 10 August 1932, at Duncan's home in Los Angeles, aged about 13. [W, BRIT (1932), NPR caption "1918-1932"]. Newspapers reported his death widely and radio programmes broke in with the news. [W]
- **Walk of Fame:** He received a star on 8 February 1960, at 1627 Vine Street. [W, Commons photo of the star]
- **Successors:** Rin Tin Tin Jr. was his son and made films and serials, including *The Adventures of Rex and Rinty* (1935). He died in 1941. The 1950s ABC TV series *The Adventures of Rin Tin Tin* (1954-59) used **different dogs**: nominally Rin Tin Tin IV, but most scenes were done by trainer Frank Barnes's dog Flame Jr. A bloodline and trademark carry on today (Rin Tin Tin XII and later). [W, BRIT]

## Disputed points, with the SAFEST wording
1. **Origin story.** It rests on Duncan's own telling. Orlean said "the story of how Rin Tin Tin was found was not always told in the same way" [CSM]. Litter size and details vary (Warner says six dogs). SAY: "According to Duncan, in September 1918 he found a shell-damaged kennel at Flirey, France, and inside, a starving mother and her newborn puppies."
2. **"Saved Warner Bros. from bankruptcy."** Commentators repeat this often, but no balance sheet proves it. Box-office figures for *Where the North Begins* also disagree. SAY: "His films became some of the young Warner Bros. studio's most dependable hits. Studio insiders called him 'the mortgage lifter', and he is often credited with helping keep Warner Bros. afloat."
3. **Birth date.** Say "September 1918" (see above).
4. **Number of films.** Say "more than two dozen" or "around 27".
5. **Burial.** Older accounts, including Orlean's, say Duncan buried him in his yard and later had his remains moved to the Cimetière des Chiens pet cemetery at Asnières-sur-Seine near Paris, where a grave bears his name. Wikipedia, citing historian Paul Koudounaris (2024), now says that grave is a different German shepherd and the original's resting place is unknown. SAY: "Duncan buried him at home. Where he rests today is disputed. A famous grave near Paris bears his name, but some historians now doubt it is his." Do NOT state the Paris grave as fact.
6. **How he died.** Wikipedia says Duncan heard an unusual bark and found him dying. This comes through Orlean only, so attribute it: "according to Duncan's account".
7. **The Duncan quote** "crept right into a lonesome place in my life and had become a part of me" is single-source (Maclean's quoting Duncan's writings via Orlean). Use only with on-screen attribution "Duncan later wrote", or leave it out.

## Myths to correct on screen
- **"Rin Tin Tin won the first Best Actor Oscar and the Academy re-ran the vote."** FALSE. The surviving 1928 ballots, all signed, are in the Academy's Margaret Herrick Library. Bruce Davis, the Academy's former executive director, checked them: "not a single one of them mentions a dog." The joke started with Darryl Zanuck at Warner, and Jack Warner put the dog on his nomination ballot as a gag. The award went to Emil Jannings. [WRAP, W] Orlean's 2011 book repeated the myth [WRAP, W].
- **"He died in Jean Harlow's arms."** A press invention. He died at Duncan's home. [W citing ORL pp. 109-112; BRIT/NPR give the year only]
- **"The TV Rin Tin Tin (1950s) was the same dog."** FALSE. The original died in 1932, and later dogs used the name (see Successors).
- **"Rin Tin Tin was a war dog."** He was a puppy rescued during the war, not a military working dog.
- **"Saved Warner Bros." stated as hard fact.** Use the hedged wording above.

## Emotional peak moments (true)
1. A bombed kennel on the front line, a starving mother and blind newborn puppies, and a lonely soldier, himself raised partly in an orphanage, who could not leave them. [W, MAC, PM]
2. Nanette, the sister puppy, dies of pneumonia just after reaching America, so Duncan has only Rinty left. [W/ORL, NPR]
3. 1932: the most famous dog in the world dies at home with the man who found him. Radio breaks into its programmes to announce it. [W]
- Bonus: Rinty breaks a leg in an accident; Duncan sets it and nurses him for months [W citing ORL], and soon afterwards he becomes a star.

## Hook (true)
"September 1918. In a shell-wrecked kennel on the Western Front, an American soldier found a starving mother dog and five puppies too young to open their eyes. Five years later, one of those puppies was one of the biggest movie stars in America."
Alt: "The dog who 'won' the very first Oscar never did. The real story is stranger, and sadder."
