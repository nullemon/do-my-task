# The Dinosaur-Killer Asteroid, Seen From the Ground (YouTube Short)

The vertical cut is 1080×1920 at 30 fps and 39.4 s long. There is also a 16:9 recomposition at 1920×1080. It uses one
**locked** tripod shot of the user's `A_park.png` (transparent sky), which stands in for "if you were standing about
1,000 km from Chicxulub". The look follows the bold VFX style of `../other-suns/` (rainbow sun beams, coloured impact
beams, chromatic bloom and shockwave rings, big readouts, strong relighting of the park per phase). One big orange word
is shown at a time, synced to the voice. A T+ counter at the top shows how much time is being compressed. There are no
disclaimer captions (user rule).

## Narration (96 words, Kokoro am_michael @ 1.05)
> What if you saw the dinosaur-killer asteroid hit, from a thousand kilometers away? A new star appears. Ten kilometers
> wide, at twenty kilometers a second. It strikes beyond the horizon. A flash brighter than the Sun. A fireball climbs
> into space. Minutes later, the ground heaves. Magnitude ten. Molten debris rains back from space. The sky glows
> red-hot, maybe hot enough to ignite forests. An hour in, the blast wave hits. Then soot blocks the Sun for years.
> Three quarters of all species vanish. And the asteroid that wiped out the dinosaurs... is why we're here.

## Science notes and sources
- **Impactor:** about 10 km across (published estimates are roughly 10–15 km), at about 20 km/s. Collins et al. 2020
  (*Nature Communications*, "A steeply-inclined trajectory for the Chicxulub impact") model a ~45–60° impact. The
  readouts say "~".
- **Energy:** about 10²³–10²⁴ J; the commonly quoted figure is ~4×10²³ J ≈ 100 teratonnes of TNT = **~100 million
  megatons**. Hiroshima was ~15 kt (6.3×10¹³ J), so that is ~6 billion Hiroshimas (range ~1.6–16 billion): "billions of
  Hiroshimas".
- **Seen from ~1,000 km:** at 20 km/s the asteroid crosses the ~100 km of atmosphere in a few seconds. Earth's curvature
  hides anything below about 80 km altitude at 1,000 km (d²/2R ≈ 78 km). So the bolide drops behind the horizon, and the
  fireball and the vapour/ejecta plume, which rose far above that and partly into space, appear above it.
- **Shaking:** the seismic energy is estimated at ~10²³ J (Bermúdez et al., GSA 2022: about 50,000× the 2004 Sumatra
  earthquake), and is commonly quoted as a magnitude ~10–11 equivalent. Seismic waves cover 1,000 km in about 2–5
  minutes (P waves ~8 km/s, surface waves ~3–4 km/s).
- **Ejecta re-entry:** distal ejecta (melt spherules) re-entered worldwide over tens of minutes to hours and heated the
  upper atmosphere. Whether the infrared pulse ignited global wildfires is **debated**: it was proposed by Melosh et al.
  1990 and Robertson et al. 2004, and questioned by Belcher et al. 2003/2009 and Goldin & Melosh 2009 (spherule
  self-shielding). Hence "maybe hot enough to ignite forests".
- **Air blast:** sound and the air blast travel at roughly 0.3–0.4 km/s, so at ~1,000 km they arrive in roughly 45–60
  minutes. That is after the light, the seismic waves and the start of the ejecta rain: "an hour in, the blast wave hits".
- **Impact winter:** soot (from fires and the target rock) and sulfate aerosols (from the gypsum-rich target) blocked
  sunlight for months to years. The narration says "for years". The readout says "months to years".
- **Extinction:** about 75% of species disappeared at the end-Cretaceous, including all non-avian dinosaurs. Birds
  survived, so the readout says "non-bird dinosaurs". Mammals radiated afterwards, which led to primates and us.

## Liberties (deliberately not captioned, per the user)
- Time is compressed (from seconds to a year in about 23 s). The counter states it as a fact, not as a disclaimer.
- The plume is drawn very large and in colourful style. Its exact appearance at 1,000 km is uncertain. The coloured
  beams, chromatic rings, spikes and trail colours are artistic devices.
- The park is modern: it stands in for "if you were standing there". The bolide is shown as a visible "new star" a few
  seconds before entry. In reality it would be visible as a bright point for a while before that, depending on lighting.
- The camera stays locked apart from a few frames of jolt at the quake and the air blast (the ground itself moving).
- Ejecta streak directions are stylised (they radiate away from the impact side).
