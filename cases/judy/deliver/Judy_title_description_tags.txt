==================== YOUTUBE DESCRIPTION ====================
Judy was an English pointer born in Shanghai in 1936 who became the mascot of the Royal Navy gunboats HMS Gnat and HMS Grasshopper on the Yangtze. After Grasshopper was bombed in February 1942, she found fresh water for the stranded survivors, trekked across Sumatra, and was captured. In the prison camp at Medan, RAF airman Frank Williams shared his rice with her, and she was registered as prisoner of war 81A: the only dog known to have been officially registered as a POW in WWII.

She survived the torpedoing of a prison ship, three years of camps, and came home to receive the PDSA Dickin Medal in 1946. The film in this video is real 1930s–40s newsreel and National Archives footage of the places and events in her story; her real photos are labelled.

FACT-CHECK NOTES
• "Only dog known to have been officially registered as a POW" — the word "officially" matters.
• Who registered her, and when, differs between sources; we follow Frank Williams's own account and say so.
• The prison-ship death toll varies widely between sources (about 150–500); we say "many did not survive".
• GOV.UK gives her death year as 1960; her grave inscription and The National Archives say February 1950.
• Frank Williams (RAF) and Leonard Williams (Royal Navy, who said "a lot of people owe their lives to Judy") were not related.
• Pointer footage shows other English pointers and is labelled "not Judy".
