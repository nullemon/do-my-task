## Confirmed facts
- **Dog**: Smoky, female Yorkshire terrier, about 4 lb (1.8 kg), about 7 in tall. [CLEMAG][WIKI][MIL]
- **Found**: abandoned in a jungle foxhole in New Guinea, early 1944. Wikipedia says February 1944; Wynne (CLEMAG) says he bought her in March 1944. SAY: "in early 1944". [NWW2M][AWM][CLEMAG][WIKI]
- At first some thought she belonged to Japanese troops, but she understood neither Japanese nor English commands. [NWW2M]
- **Bought**: by Cpl. William "Bill" Wynne (b. 1922, Cleveland, Ohio) for two Australian pounds; the seller wanted the money for a poker game (Wynne's own account). [CLEMAG][WIKI][AWM]
- **Wynne's unit**: US Army Air Forces, Fifth Air Force, 26th Photo Reconnaissance Squadron; aerial photographer. [AWM][CLEMAG][NASA]
- Wynne bathed her in his helmet (origin of the famous "dog in a helmet" photo). [AWM][WIKI]
- **Service**: credited with 12 air-sea rescue / photo-reconnaissance missions; endured about 150 air raids on New Guinea and a typhoon at Okinawa. [NWW2M][WIKI][PT] (NASA says Wynne himself flew 13 combat missions.)
- **Mascot fame**: Wynne entered her photo in a Yank Down Under magazine mascot contest; she was named "Champion Mascot in the Southwest Pacific Area," 1944. [WIKI][CLEMAG-adjacent accounts] (Exact magazine title spelled "Yank Down Under" in Wikipedia; treat date July 1944 as approximate.)
- **Hospital visits (therapy origin)**: July 1944 Wynne had dengue fever at Nadzab, New Guinea; friends brought Smoky to the tent hospital and nurses borrowed her to cheer wounded men in other wards. Later they visited Army and Navy hospitals in Brisbane, Australia. [CLEMAG][PT][WIKI] Wynne quote (CLEMAG, verbatim): "GIs in wheelchairs would follow us from ward to ward."
- **The culvert (key moment)**: after US landings at Lingayen Gulf, Luzon, Philippines (landings began 9 Jan 1945), engineers/Signal Corps needed a telegraph/communications wire under a newly built or captured airstrip without digging up the runway. The culvert was about **70 ft (21 m) long, about 8 in (20 cm) in diameter**, partly filled with sand/soil. [NWW2M][AWM][CLEMAG][WIKI]
  - Wynne tied a string/kite line to her collar and called her from the far end; she crawled through in minutes; the wire was then pulled through. [AWM][CLEMAG][NWW2M]
  - Wynne (CLEMAG, verbatim): "When she was about 10 feet inside the pipe, the string got caught" ... "I called and pleaded with her to keep going."
  - Digging the runway would have taken about **three days**; Smoky did it in minutes. [NWW2M][WIKI citing Wynne's book]
  - Reward: a steak dinner (Sgt. Bob Gapp, per CLEMAG — single source; fine as color, attribute to Wynne).
- **After the war**: home to Cleveland; page one of the Cleveland Press, 7 Dec 1945 [WIKI]; performed tricks on TV and in shows (Cleveland TV, "Castles in the Air" on WKYC Ch. 3 per WIKI); visited veterans' hospitals. [NWW2M][CLEMAG][NASA]
- Wynne married Margie Roberts, 28 Sept 1946; nine children. [CLEMAG]
- **Death**: 21 Feb 1957, in her sleep, at the Wynnes' home in Parma Heights, Ohio, about 14 years old. [CLEMAG][WIKI] (A few sites say 1955 or Feb 20: wrong/minor.)
- **Burial**: Wynne buried her in a WWII .30-caliber ammunition box in the Rocky River Reservation, Cleveland Metroparks (Lakewood, Ohio). [WIKI][METRO-adjacent; Atlas Obscura/Roadside America concur]
- **Memorial**: bronze statue of Smoky sitting in a GI helmet on top of a stack of books, by Susan Bahary, dedicated **11 Nov 2005** (Veterans Day) at that spot, "to Smoky and the dogs of all wars." [WIKI][BAHARY][METRO]
- Other memorials/honors: Royal Brisbane and Women's Hospital, Herston, Queensland ("Smoky, the Dog in the Helmet") [AWM][WIKI]; AKC Museum of the Dog, National Museum of the US Air Force, others listed on Wikipedia (single-source list; do not enumerate on screen without checking).
- **PDSA Certificate for Animal Bravery or Devotion**, posthumous, April 2011, for the January 1945 Lingayen act — first WWII animal to get it. [WIKI][PDSA release via secondary sites]
- **Animals in War & Peace Distinguished Service Medal**, 2022. [WIKI][MIL]
- Philippine Post (PHLPost) put Smoky on a 2023 "Historic Dogs of the Philippines" stamp. [Wikimedia Commons file]
- **Bill Wynne** died **19 April 2021, aged 99** (Ohio). Later a Plain Dealer photographer and NASA/NACA photographer. [NASA][WIKI]

## Disputed points: SAFEST wording
- **"First therapy dog"**: Cleveland Metroparks says "the world's first therapy dog on record"; this traces to an Animal Planet program. Psychology Today calls her "one contender." SAY: "often called the first therapy dog on record" or "one of the earliest known therapy dogs." Never "the first therapy dog in history" as plain fact.
- **Pipe size**: 70 ft long, 8 in diameter is consistent across NWW2M, AWM, CLEMAG. With sand inside, "only a few inches of clearance in places." SAY: "a pipe about 70 feet long and just 8 inches wide."
- **Days saved / lives saved**: "about three days of digging" is safe (Wynne + NWW2M). "Saved 250 men and 40 planes" is Wynne-derived (via eagleid.com / Psych Today estimate): SAY "kept the runway, and the planes on it, in action, and spared the crews from digging under fire" or attribute: "Wynne said it spared about 250 men from the dangerous digging." Avoid "saved 250 lives."
- **Date of culvert**: PDSA says January 1945; CLEMAG says February 1945. SAY: "early 1945."
- **Date found**: Feb vs March 1944. SAY "early 1944."
- **Original owner**: AWM names Lt. Grace Guderian, US Army nurse; CLEMAG says nurse Grace Heidenreich (married name) called in 1957 saying she lost a Yorkie at a USO show in New Guinea, and the odds it was Smoky were "excellent" — not proven. SAY: "Years later, a former Army nurse came forward. She believed Smoky might have been the little dog she lost in New Guinea." Do not state as fact.
- **Dr. Charles Mayo let Smoky make rounds / sleep in Wynne's hospital bed**: Wikipedia + Psych Today, ultimately Wynne's account. Use with "according to Wynne."
- **Born in Kerema / Brisbane, Dec 1943**: unclear; skip.
- **"Missions"**: say "credited with 12 missions" (not "flew combat").

## Myths / popular errors to correct
- "She saved 250 lives": not documented; it was about saving digging work and keeping planes operational.
- "Found in the Philippines": No, found in New Guinea; the culvert was in the Philippines (Luzon).
- "Military working dog / official war dog": She was a privately owned mascot, not an enlisted or K-9 Corps dog.
- "Died 1955": She died 21 Feb 1957.
- "The helmet statue is her grave marker in Cleveland city": it is in the Rocky River Reservation (Cleveland Metroparks) in Lakewood.
- Viral images of other Yorkies captioned as Smoky. Only use verified Wynne-era photos (copyrighted, see media.json).

## Emotional peak moments (all true)
1. A tiny, starving 4-lb dog found alone in a jungle foxhole, sold for two pounds so a soldier could get back to his poker game.
2. Inside the pipe: the string snags 10 feet in, dust everywhere, and Wynne at the far end calling and pleading with her to keep coming, until two eyes appear in the dark.
3. Wynne sick with dengue fever: nurses borrow Smoky, and wounded GIs in wheelchairs follow her from ward to ward.
4. 1957: Wynne buries her in a WWII ammo box; 48 years later a statue of her in his helmet stands over the spot (2005), and Wynne lived to see it, dying in 2021 at 99.
