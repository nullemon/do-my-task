## Confirmed facts
- **Dog**: Bretagne (pronounced "Brittany"), female golden retriever, AKC-registered. [CSM][AKC]
- **Born**: 25 August 1999 (CSM: "would have turned 17 on August 25"). Met handler at 8 weeks old in 1999. [CSM][SMITH]
- **Handler/owner**: Denise Corliss, electrician by trade and volunteer firefighter (later Captain) with the Cy-Fair Volunteer Fire Department, Harris County, Texas (Houston area). [SMITH][911M]
- **Team**: Texas Task Force 1 (TX-TF1), the Texas urban search-and-rescue team (FEMA US&R; run by Texas A&M Engineering Extension Service). [AKC][SMITH][CBS]
- **Training**: started formal search training around age one; trained 20 to 30 hours a week; certified as a FEMA search dog around age two; trained at Disaster City, College Station, Texas. [SMITH][AKC][CSM]
- **Ground Zero**: her FIRST deployment. About 2 years old. Worked 12-hour shifts searching the rubble at the World Trade Center site. Duration: "about 10 days" (CBS) / "nearly 12 hours a day for two weeks" (CSM). SAY: "for about two weeks" or "for days on end" — avoid an exact count. [CBS][CSM][SMITH][AKC]
- Roughly 300 dogs/search teams worked the 9/11 sites (estimate). SAY "about 300 search dogs." [SMITH][FOX6][AKC15][911M citing Houston Chronicle]
- **Comfort role**: firefighters and rescue workers came up to pet her and talk; Corliss said Bretagne went to a firefighter, lay down next to him and put her head on his lap. Corliss (Today show, via FOX6, verbatim): "She went right to that firefighter and laid down next to him and put her head on his lap." [FOX6][CSM]
- Corliss quotes (verbatim, SMITH): "I really believed we could find somebody — anybody! — if we could just get to the right void space" and (CBS, to BarkPost 2015): "We were there to try to find survivors." Avoid her follow-up line about remains (too graphic).
- **Later deployments**: Hurricane Katrina (2005), Hurricane Rita (2005), Hurricane Ivan (2004), and other disasters. [AKC][SMITH][CSM]
- **Retirement**: from disaster search at age 9 (AKC, SMITH, CBS); 911M and FOX6 say 10. SAY: "around age nine." [AKC][SMITH]
- **Reading dog**: in retirement she went to a local elementary school, where first graders and special-needs children read aloud to her; also helped train younger search dogs. [AKC][SMITH][FOX6] Cy-Fair FD described her as offering children a "non-judgmental ear and a soft paw." [CSM]
- **2014**: returned with Corliss to the 9/11 Memorial & Museum; a cobblestone was dedicated in their names. [911M] Corliss (CNN 2014, via CSM): "She walked to the edge of it and she stopped – turned – and she came back down."
- 2014: finalist, American Humane Association Hero Dog Awards. [SMITH][WT14]
- **16th birthday, Aug/Sept 2015**: BarkPost "Dog's Best Day" in New York City: suite overlooking Central Park, name on a Times Square billboard, surprise party with former TX-TF1 teammates. [CBS][SMITH][AKC15]
- **Death**: Monday **6 June 2016**, age 16, euthanized at Fairfield Animal Hospital in Cypress, Texas, after kidney failure, failing joints and three days of not eating. [CBS][CSM][FOX6][SMITH]
- Corliss (verbatim, SMITH/CSM via NBC): "She was really anxious last night and she just wanted to be with me"
- **The salute**: about two dozen firefighters and search-and-rescue volunteers (Cy-Fair FD and TX-TF1) lined the path into the clinic and saluted as she walked in with Corliss; she came out draped in an American flag. [SMITH][CSM][FOX6][HC]
- Capt. David Padovan, Cy-Fair VFD (verbatim, SMITH): "This was a very small way for us to pay tribute to a dog who truly has been a hero"
- Cy-Fair VFD statement (verbatim, 911M): "Bretagne was a civil servant, a hero and is family."
- Her body went to Texas A&M for a necropsy, part of the long-running study of 9/11 search dogs. [SMITH]
- **AKC honor**: posthumous AKC Humane Fund/"Empire State" recognition, accepted by Corliss in NYC, March 2017. AKC VP Gina DiNardo (verbatim): "Bretagne was a dedicated, courageous and loyal working dog whose commitment and bravery has not gone unnoticed." [AKC]

## Disputed points: SAFEST wording
- **"Last surviving 9/11 search dog"**: Every outlet hedged: "last known" (CBS, Smithsonian, 9/11 Memorial), "believed to be" (Washington Times), "thought to be" (Smithsonian). Penn Vet's Dr. Cynthia Otto, who tracked the 9/11 dogs, reportedly said there is no way to be sure. SAY: "believed to be the last known surviving search dog from Ground Zero." Never "the last 9/11 dog" as plain fact.
- **"Guard of honor"**: sources describe firefighters lining the path and saluting. SAY: "firefighters lined the path and saluted her." "Honor guard" is acceptable as description, not as an official ceremony title.
- **Days at Ground Zero**: 10 days vs two weeks. SAY "about two weeks" or "day after day, in 12-hour shifts."
- **Retirement age**: 9 vs 10. SAY "around nine."
- **Did she find survivors?** No living survivors were found by search dogs after the first day; do NOT imply she rescued anyone alive. SAY: "She was there to search for survivors."

## Myths / popular errors to correct
- **Wrong photos**: the widely shared FEMA photo of a golden retriever asleep in an orange stretcher at Ground Zero, and the photo of a golden being carried across rubble on a pulley (that dog is Riley, Pennsylvania Task Force 1), are often captioned as Bretagne. Neither is confirmed to be her. Do not caption any Ground Zero dog photo as Bretagne unless the source names her.
- **Wrong task force**: some posts say she was with Pennsylvania Task Force 1. She was Texas Task Force 1 (AKC, Smithsonian, CBS).
- "She rescued survivors from the towers": no.
- "She died on 9/11 anniversary" / "died at 17": she died 6 June 2016 at 16.
- "Therapy dog at Ground Zero": she was a search dog who also comforted workers; her later formal role was a school reading dog.

## Emotional peak moments (all true)
1. Ground Zero, 2001: a two-year-old golden on her first-ever mission, searching 12-hour shifts; exhausted firefighters stop to pet her, and she lays her head on one firefighter's lap.
2. 2014: back at the 9/11 site 13 years later; at the memorial she walks to the edge, stops, turns and comes back.
3. Retirement: children who struggle to read aloud read to her, a "non-judgmental ear and a soft paw."
4. 6 June 2016: her handler walks her one last time into the vet as two lines of firefighters salute; she leaves under an American flag.
