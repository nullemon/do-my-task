### 7. Hachikō: "He Waited 9 Years at the Train Station"

- Akita, Tokyo. His owner, Professor Hidesaburō Ueno, died at work on May 21, 1925.
- Hachikō went back to Shibuya Station every day until he died on March 8, 1935.
- **Flags:**
  - Many "real photos" and postcards online are fake or staged.
  - Check where Hachikō first arrived in Tokyo; sources disagree.
  - Hachikō is male.
- **Footage:**
  - low to medium: real 1930s photos, the statues (Shibuya; the University of Tokyo statue of the reunion with Ueno), and his preserved body at the National Museum of Nature and Science
  - avoid clips from the movies (*Hachi*, 2009; the 1987 Japanese film)
- **Sources:** Wikipedia "Hachikō"; Japan Times; NHK; University of Tokyo.

### 8. Capitán: "He Lived at His Owner's Grave for 11 Years"

- German shepherd mix, Villa Carlos Paz, Argentina. His owner, Miguel Guzmán, died in 2006.
- Cemetery staff first saw Capitán there in January 2007. The newspaper La Voz confirmed the story with the cemetery director.
- He died in February 2018.
- **Flag:** the viral photo is of a DIFFERENT dog. Use only footage from La Voz or Argentine TV.
- **Sources:** PolitiFact; ABC News (2012); La Voz del Interior.

### 9. Sergeant Stubby: "The Stray Who Became a War Hero"

- A stray from New Haven who served with the 102nd Infantry, 26th "Yankee" Division, in WWI.
- He served in 17 battles and was wounded by shrapnel and gas. He warned soldiers of gas attacks, and is said to have helped catch a German spy.
- His remains are at the Smithsonian (National Museum of American History).
- **Flags:**
  - His rank was never official, so present "Sergeant" as a nickname.
  - The spy story rests on period accounts.
  - Don't use clips from the 2018 animated film.
- **Sources:** Smithsonian; the National WWI Museum; U.S. Air Force (Beale); Connecticut State Library.

### 10. Patron: "The Tiny Dog Who Found 200 Bombs"

- A Jack Russell terrier with Ukraine's State Emergency Service (Chernihiv), who works with his handler, Mykhailo Iliev.
- President Zelensky awarded him a medal in May 2022, during Canadian PM Trudeau's visit.
- **Flags:**
  - Counts vary: 90 → 200+ → more. Say "credited with".
  - The war setting is sensitive, so keep it neutral and uplifting.
- **Sources:** NPR; Reuters; AP; Ukrainian SES (ДСНС) official channels.
