# Hachikō: verified facts (checked Nov 2026)
Sources: Wikipedia "Hachikō" (cites Itoh 2017); nippon.com jg00137; U Tokyo Fac. of Agriculture (en.a.u-tokyo.ac.jp/hachi_ueno_hp); Time Out Tokyo / Toyo Keizai;
U Tokyo vet pathology PDF (vm.a.u-tokyo.ac.jp/byouri/HPpict/hachiko.pdf); AP via 6abc (2011); Japan Times 2011-03-04; Kyodo; Mainichi 2016-05-20; Tokyo Weekender 2022.

- Born Nov 1923 near Odate, Akita (Nov 10 is the date celebrated). Sent by train to Tokyo (Ueno Station) in Jan 1924, ~50 days old, about 20 h.
- Owner: Prof. Hidesaburō Ueno, agricultural engineering pioneer, Tokyo Imperial University. They lived together ~16 months.
- May 21, 1925: Ueno died suddenly at the university after a faculty meeting (NOT at the station). Say "a sudden stroke".
- Routine of meeting Ueno at Shibuya: from family recollections (Kobayashi), so say "according to the family".
- After: lived with relatives, then from summer 1925 with the gardener Kikusaburō Kobayashi in Tomigaya; passed between homes; kept returning to Shibuya Station.
- Waited about 9 years 10 months (say "almost ten years").
- Hirokichi Saitō (Japanese dog preservation society) first saw him in July 1928; Tokyo Asahi Shimbun article Oct 4, 1932 made him famous.
- Some people mistreated him (kicked him, drew on his face); others fed him. Say both.
- First statue by Teru Andō, unveiled April 21, 1934, Hachikō attended. Removed Oct 1944, melted for the war effort. Replaced Aug 1948 by his son Takeshi Andō.
- Died March 8, 1935, on a street near Shibuya Station, aged 11. 2011 U Tokyo study: terminal cancer + heartworm. Yakitori skewers in his stomach did NOT kill him.
- Funeral; monument beside Ueno's grave at Aoyama Cemetery. Mounted pelt at the National Museum of Nature and Science (Ueno). Organs kept at U Tokyo.
- One ear drooped in later years (single source; statue shows it).
- March 2015: U Tokyo statue of Hachi leaping to greet Ueno, 80 years after his death (statue under copyright; avoid photos of it).
- 2016: part of Yaeko Sakano's (Ueno's partner) ashes were interred with Ueno and Hachikō.
- Film: brief appearance in the 1934 film "Arupusu taishō". Bark: 78 rpm record restored and aired in 1994 (Washington Post 1994-06-03).

MYTHS to correct: Ueno died at the station; skewers killed him; statue melted into bullets; fake photos and birth certificate.
