==================== YOUTUBE DESCRIPTION ====================
In 1924, a white Akita puppy rode a train for about twenty hours to Tokyo, to live with Professor Hidesaburō Ueno. Every evening, the family recalled, Hachi went to Shibuya Station to meet him. In May 1925 the professor died suddenly at the university, and never came back. Hachi kept returning to the station for almost ten years, until his death in March 1935.

This is the true story, checked against museum, university and news sources. It also covers what the movies and viral posts get wrong: Ueno did not die at the station, and the yakitori skewers found in Hachi's stomach did not kill him. A 2011 University of Tokyo study found cancer and heartworm.

FACT-CHECK NOTES
• "9 years": Ueno died May 21, 1925; Hachi died March 8, 1935, so the wait was about 9 years 10 months. We say "9 years" in the title and "almost ten years" in the narration.
• Ueno died at the university, not at the station (a common myth). Sources differ on the exact cause ("cerebral hemorrhage" vs "aneurysm"), so we say "a sudden stroke".
• The daily station routine comes from the family's recollections; we say so on screen.
• Yakitori skewers were found in his stomach, but the 2011 University of Tokyo study found cancer and heartworm caused his death.
• The first statue (Teru Andō, April 21, 1934) was melted down for the war effort. Takeshi Andō made the replacement in 1948.
• Many "Hachikō photos" online are other Akitas or film stills. Every photo in this video comes from the Wikimedia Commons Hachikō categories.
• The train route on the map is approximate.
