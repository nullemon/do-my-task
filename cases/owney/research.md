## CONFIRMED (2+ sources)
- **Who:** a scruffy terrier mix (a "mutt") who became the unofficial mascot of the U.S. Railway Mail Service (RMS) for about nine years, 1888 to 1897. [USPS facts; Smithsonian Mag; NPM]
- **Origin:** he first appears at the **Albany, New York post office in 1888** (USPS says autumn 1888). Postal workers let him stay and named him **Owney**. [USPS facts; USPS blog; NPM]
- **He loved the mailbags.** He slept on them and followed them onto mail wagons, then onto the mail trains (Railway Post Office cars), first across New York State and then across the country. [NPM; Smithsonian Mag]
- **Collar and tag:** the Albany clerks gave him a collar with a tag reading "Owney, Post Office, Albany, N.Y." [USPS facts; Wikipedia/Pope]
- **Tags:** clerks and others along the routes added tags, medals and tokens: railway baggage checks, hotel keys, dog licences and gifts. [NPM; USPS facts] The NPM holds **372** of them. [Wikipedia, citing Smithsonian]
- **The harness:** the collar got too heavy, so **Postmaster General John Wanamaker** gave him a harness or jacket to spread the weight. [USPS facts; Smithsonian Mag; NPM]
- **Miles:** "**more than 140,000 miles**" [USPS blog; Wikipedia gives 143,000, citing Pope/NPM]. Say "more than 140,000 miles".
- **World trip, 1895:** left **Tacoma, Washington, on August 19, 1895**, aboard the mail steamer **Victoria** (Northern Pacific line), with mailbags. The trip was a publicity effort for Tacoma, arranged by the Tacoma postmaster. [NPM; Smithsonian Mag; Wikipedia; Roanoke Daily Times 1896]
  - Countries and places: Japan, China (Hong Kong, Shanghai, Foochow), Singapore, Suez/Port Said, Algiers, the Azores; earlier travels also took him to Mexico and Canada. [USPS facts lists Mexico, Canada, Japan, China, Singapore, Suez, Algiers, Azores; Roanoke Daily Times 1896]
  - Back in New York in late December 1895, then across the US by train to Tacoma. [Wikipedia; Roanoke 1896; Smithsonian Torch]
- **Last months:** by 1897 he was old, had been ill, and was sometimes irritable. A St. Louis clerk took him in, but he would not stay put. [NPM; Smithsonian Mag; Wikipedia/Pope]
- **Death:** **June 11, 1897, in Toledo, Ohio.** After he bit a mail clerk, he was put down on the local postmaster's orders. [USPS facts; NPM; Smithsonian Mag; Wikipedia]
- **Preserved:** railway mail clerks raised the money themselves to have him preserved by a taxidermist. He was displayed at Post Office Department headquarters in Washington, D.C. [USPS facts; NPM; Smithsonian Mag]
- **Afterwards:** shown at the 1904 St. Louis World's Fair [NPM; Wikipedia]; transferred to the **Smithsonian in 1911** [USPS facts; NPM; Smithsonian Mag says 1912, see below]; shown at the National Museum of American History from 1964 to 1992; at the **National Postal Museum since 1993**, next to a replica Railway Post Office car. [NPM; USPS facts]
- **2011:** a major conservation by taxidermist **Paul Rhymer** (about a month; a stand-in nicknamed "Phony Owney" filled in) [Smithsonian Mag; Washington Post via Wikipedia]. **USPS "Owney the Postal Dog" Forever stamp issued July 27, 2011.** [USPS blog; NPM; Wikipedia] The stamp artist Bill Bond worked from the mounted Owney. [Wikipedia, citing USPS]

## MYTHS AND POPULAR ERRORS TO CORRECT ON SCREEN
- "He was a stray who just wandered in." The Smithsonian's research says he probably had an owner, a postal clerk.
- "He wore over 1,000 tags at once." No: the harness took the overflow, and only a fraction could be worn at a time.
- "He was a purebred terrier." He was a mutt (terrier mix).
- "He died of old age." He was put down in Toledo (keep it gentle).
- Commons and Flickr captions still repeat the "stray, cold night" version. Don't read them on screen.

## EMOTIONAL PEAK MOMENTS (true)
1. A little dog sleeps on the mailbags; when they leave, he follows them onto the wagons and onto the trains.
2. Clerks across America each add a tag, until the collar is so heavy that the Postmaster General has a harness made for him.
3. Aug 19, 1895: a mutt walks up the gangway in Tacoma with his own bag and letters of introduction, and sails around the world. [Roanoke Daily Times 1896]
4. The clerks loved him enough to pay out of their own pockets to preserve him, so he could stay with the mail forever.
5. More than 110 years later, the Postal Service puts him on a stamp (2011).

## HOOK IDEA (true)
"In 1895, a scruffy mutt with no ticket sailed around the world... carrying the U.S. Mail." / Text: "THIS MUTT TRAVELED THE WORLD... BY MAIL."
Alternative: "He rode more than 140,000 miles on mail trains, and nobody owned him."
