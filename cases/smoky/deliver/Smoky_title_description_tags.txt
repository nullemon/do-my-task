==================== YOUTUBE DESCRIPTION ====================
In early 1944, American soldiers found a tiny Yorkshire terrier alone in a jungle foxhole in New Guinea. Cpl. Bill Wynne, an Army Air Forces photographer, bought her for two Australian pounds and named her Smoky. She is credited with 12 missions, survived about 150 air raids and a typhoon, and visited wounded soldiers in hospital wards. She is often called the first therapy dog on record.

In early 1945 at Lingayen Gulf in the Philippines, engineers needed a telegraph wire run through a pipe about 70 feet long and 8 inches wide under an airfield. Smoky crawled through it with a string tied to her collar, saving days of dangerous digging. The film in this video is real 1943–45 U.S. Army and Navy footage from the National Archives; Yorkie clips of other dogs are labelled.

FACT-CHECK NOTES
• "Saved 250 lives" is not documented; she saved days of digging and kept the airfield working.
• She was found in New Guinea, not the Philippines; the pipe was on Luzon.
• She was a privately owned mascot, not an official war dog.
• "First therapy dog" is how she is often described; we say "often called".
• Personal details (the price, the poker game, the pleading) come from Bill Wynne's own account.
• No free photo of the real Smoky exists; Yorkshire terrier clips show other dogs and are labelled "not Smoky".
