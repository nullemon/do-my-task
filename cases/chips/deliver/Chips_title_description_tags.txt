==================== YOUTUBE DESCRIPTION ====================
In 1942, the Wren family of Pleasantville, New York, lent their German shepherd mix Chips to the U.S. Army through Dogs for Defense. He guarded the Casablanca Conference, and on 10 July 1943, near Licata in Sicily, he broke free from his handler and charged a machine-gun nest. Four Italian soldiers surrendered, and that night he is credited with helping capture ten more.

He was awarded the Silver Star and the Purple Heart. Then came the backlash: the Army ruled that no more medals would go to animals. Seventy-five years later, in 2018, Chips finally received the PDSA Dickin Medal in London, with John Wren, the boy who welcomed him home, in the audience. The film in this video is real 1942–43 U.S. Army, Navy and newsreel footage from the National Archives.

FACT-CHECK NOTES
• The soldiers in the machine-gun nest were Italian, not German.
• Chips was a mix (German shepherd–husky type), not a purebred.
• The ten prisoners that night are "credited" to Chips and Rowell in the sources.
• Whether his medals were formally taken back is disputed: modern sources say revoked; 1944 reports say he could keep them but no more medals would go to animals. We say the Army ruled no more medals for animals.
• The Eisenhower nip is presented as "the story goes" (the Army calls it a rumor).
• He died in 1946; sources differ on the cause, so we don't state it.
• Training footage shows other WWII Army dogs and is labelled "not Chips" on screen.
