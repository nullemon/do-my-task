==================== YOUTUBE DESCRIPTION ====================
In 1917 a brindle stray wandered into the camp of the 102nd Infantry at Yale. A young soldier, Robert Conroy, named him Stubby and smuggled him to France in a ship's coal bin. Stubby survived gas, was wounded at Seicheprey in April 1918, visited wounded men in hospital, and came home to parades, a gold medal from General Pershing, and a lifetime YMCA membership. Today he stands in the Smithsonian.

The film in this video is real 1917–18 U.S. Army Signal Corps footage of Stubby's own division, the 26th "Yankee" Division, from the National Archives. We also set the record straight: "Sergeant" was a nickname, he never received a Purple Heart, and he was a mascot, not an official war dog.

FACT-CHECK NOTES
• "Sergeant" was never an official rank (Ann Bausum: "promoted by the internet").
• No Purple Heart: the modern medal was created in 1932, six years after Stubby died.
• The German "spy" capture rests on newspaper accounts of the day; locations differ, so we present it as reported.
• "Credited with" finding wounded men; breed unknown; birth date unknown.
• The newspaper tribute quoted near the end is cited by the Smithsonian; the original paper is not identified.
