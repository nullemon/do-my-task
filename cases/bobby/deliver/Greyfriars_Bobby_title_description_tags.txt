==================== YOUTUBE DESCRIPTION ====================
For 150 years Edinburgh has told the story of Greyfriars Bobby, the little terrier who is said to have guarded his master's grave for 14 years. Here is what the records actually show: a small terrier really did live in and around Greyfriars Kirkyard in the 1860s, fed by the neighbours. In 1867 the Lord Provost paid for his dog licence and gave him a brass collar that still exists. When he died in 1872, The Scotsman printed an obituary, and a year later a statue and drinking fountain were put up in his honour.

But historian Jan Bondeson argued the legend was a business story, and that there may even have been two Bobbys. Others disagree. We look at the evidence on both sides. The footage shows Edinburgh today and on film in 1950; Bobby's real photos are labelled.

FACT-CHECK NOTES
• The 14-year vigil is a legend; no record proves it. We present it as "the story goes".
• The owner is usually named as John Gray, a police night watchman; even this is debated.
• "Auld Jock" comes from the 1912 novel and the 1961 Disney film, not from records.
• Rubbing the statue's nose is a modern tourist habit, not an old tradition.
• The two-dog theory (Bondeson, 2011) is disputed (Assarsson, 2024).
